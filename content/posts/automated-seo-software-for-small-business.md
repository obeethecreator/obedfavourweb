RankYak launched on September 2, an AI platform that handles keyword research, content planning, daily article writing, publishing, and ongoing page maintenance, with no in-house SEO team required. It's priced at $99 per site per month, or $990 per site per year, with a free trial. On paper, it's a full SEO department for the price of a couple of coffees a day. Here's what that actually means if you're a small business owner weighing it.

## What actually launched

RankYak positions itself as handling every stage of SEO, not just writing articles. It researches keywords, plans content around them, writes and publishes daily, and maintains the pages a site already has so they keep ranking instead of decaying. The pitch is specifically aimed at small businesses and agencies that can't justify a full-time SEO hire. Co-founder Lars Koole framed the whole product around that gap directly: "Most SEO tools tell you what to do and leave you to do it. Our customers do not. So we built one."

The actual workflow breaks into two distinct jobs, and the distinction matters for how much oversight you'll actually need. The keyword research and content generation side maps topics a site should cover, groups related search queries into single targets, and writes and publishes one optimized article a day, with internal links and images, directly into WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, or Duda. Separately, there's a maintenance layer: Page Guard runs daily checks for weak titles, thin descriptions, and pages losing clicks, and generates fixes, and Cannibalization Guard flags pages competing against each other for the same search and recommends which one to consolidate into. Notably, the maintenance fixes specifically require owner approval before they go live — that's a real human checkpoint built into the product on the maintenance side. Confirmed directly from the launch announcement and RankYak's own pricing page: $99/mo per site, no long-term contract required to start.

## The part worth understanding before you buy this

Here's what makes this launch more complicated than it looks: Google's own spam enforcement in 2026 has specifically targeted the exact publishing pattern a tool like this makes easy. The March 2026 spam update hit sites publishing large volumes of AI-generated pages without editorial oversight with 50 to 80% traffic drops, some losing rankings across their entire blog catalog in a single sweep. Google followed that with another spam update in August, so this isn't a one-time event, it's an active, ongoing enforcement pattern.

But here's the detail that actually matters, and it's the one most "AI content is dangerous" takes leave out: sites publishing a smaller volume of AI-assisted articles with real human editing saw traffic increases of 30 to 80% in the same period. The determining factor wasn't whether AI touched the content. It was whether a human reviewed it for real editorial value before it went live.

That's the actual question to ask about any autopilot SEO tool in 2026, RankYak included: does daily automated publishing mean daily automated publishing with no human in the loop, or does it mean automated drafting with a human checkpoint before anything goes live? Those are two very different risk profiles wearing the same marketing copy.

Worth being precise about where RankYak itself actually lands on that question, because it's not the same answer on both sides of the product. The maintenance layer, Page Guard's fixes and the cannibalization recommendations, explicitly requires your approval before anything changes on an existing page. That's a real, built-in checkpoint, not marketing language. The daily new-article generation and publishing is described as writing and publishing directly to your site, with no equivalent approval step mentioned. That's exactly the half of the product that maps onto the publishing pattern Google's spam updates have been targeting, and it's the half worth adding your own review step to, even if the maintenance side already has one built in.

## Why this actually matters for you

If you're a small business owner without the budget for an in-house SEO hire, the appeal here is real. Consistent, well-researched content published on a schedule beats the alternative most small sites actually run, which is nothing, or a burst of posts in January that trails off by March. A tool that keeps that cadence going without you personally writing every article solves a genuine problem.

But "fully automated" and "unsupervised at scale" is precisely the publishing pattern Google has been actively penalizing all year. Before you turn a tool like this loose on your site, decide how you're going to insert a real review step, even a fast one, between what it drafts and what actually goes live. The businesses that win with AI-assisted publishing in 2026 aren't the ones publishing the most. They're the ones publishing consistently with someone actually checking the work.

## What to actually do with this

If you're considering RankYak or a similar tool, treat the free trial as a chance to test your own review process, not just the tool's output quality. Set a rule for yourself before you start: every article gets a human pass before publish, no exceptions, even if that pass takes ten minutes instead of an hour. That single habit is the difference between the traffic increases Google rewarded this year and the traffic collapses it punished, using the exact same underlying technology.

Specifically, watch how the daily article generation behaves during your trial before you let it run unattended. Check whether there's a draft-review option you can enable even if it isn't the default, and if there isn't, build your own checkpoint outside the tool — a daily fifteen-minute pass over what published before you consider the day done. Let the maintenance side, Page Guard and Cannibalization Guard, run closer to its built-in approval flow, since that part already has a human gate designed into it. Treat the new-article side with more caution than the marketing copy suggests you need to.
