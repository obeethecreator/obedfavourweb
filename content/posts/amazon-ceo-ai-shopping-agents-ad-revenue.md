Every few weeks someone tells me AI agents are about to replace the entire online shopping experience, that your client's product pages need to be rewritten for robots instead of people, and that any agency not chasing this right now is already behind. Then the CEO of the one company actually running this experiment at scale gets on an earnings call and says, in plain words, that it is not happening yet.

That is exactly what happened this week. On Amazon's Q3 2026 earnings call, held Thursday, CEO Andy Jassy told investors that few customers are doing much shopping through outside AI agents, and that the experience right now is still not great nor more convenient than just going to the retailer directly. That is not a competitor talking Amazon down. That is Amazon talking about the entire category it is supposedly racing to win.

## What Did Amazon's CEO Actually Say?

Jassy was asked the question every agency managing paid media for an ecommerce client should want answered: are AI bots now shopping on behalf of customers, and are they skipping past sponsored listings on the way? His answer was that this is not happening, at least for now. Amazon's ad revenue that quarter came in at 19.6 billion dollars, up 12 percent year over year, even though it missed Wall Street's expectations for other reasons entirely.

Sit with that for a second. The company with the single biggest financial incentive to either prove or deny agent disruption just told its own investors the disruption has not shown up in the numbers.

## Then Why Did Amazon Block Meta's Muse?

Here is where it gets interesting, and where the real lesson for agencies lives. Amazon blocked Meta's AI agent, Muse, from shopping its site back in September, citing data privacy concerns, after Muse had already passed 10 million users in under two months. So outside AI agents are not moving the needle on sales, according to Amazon's own numbers, but Amazon still shut one out at the door.

That is not a contradiction. That is a platform drawing a line around who gets to sit between it and its customers, regardless of volume. Meanwhile Amazon's own built in shopping agent, Alexa for Shopping, which replaced Rufus earlier this year, has been used by more than 400 million customers over the past 12 months, and Amazon says US users spend 50 percent more per order when they use it.

Two agents. One gets blocked at the gate over privacy. The other gets pushed in front of 400 million customers and credited with lifting order size. The difference is not capability. The difference is who owns the relationship with the customer.

## What Does This Actually Change For Your Agency This Month?

Nothing about your reporting needs to change today, and that is the point worth sitting with. If you run paid media or ecommerce work for clients and someone on your team has been pushing to build a whole new service line around optimizing product pages for AI shopping agents, this is the week to pump the brakes, not accelerate.

Here is the actual read. Platform owned agents are winning adoption because the platform controls the data, the catalog, and the trust relationship. Third party agents are getting blocked at the door over exactly that same data question, no matter how fast they are growing. Until the biggest platforms finish fighting over who gets access to whose customers, "AI agent SEO" or "agentic commerce optimization" is not a channel most of your clients can actually buy results from. It is a channel some of your clients can pay you to prepare for.

Those are different pitches, and mixing them up is how an agency burns a client's budget and its own credibility on the same mistake.

## So What Should an Agency Actually Do With This?

Two things, and only two things, are worth doing with this news right now.

First, keep doing the measurement work you are already responsible for, and do it a little more carefully. If a client's analytics start showing a spike in sessions that convert strangely or bounce instantly, do not assume it is a tracking bug before you rule out agent traffic. Platforms are not reporting this cleanly yet, which means your dashboard might already have agent noise in it that nobody has named.

Second, resist the sales pitch that this changes everything right now. It does not. The platform that would benefit most from proving AI agents are reshaping commerce just told its own shareholders that is not what is happening. Jassy did not say this to protect Amazon's ad business out of generosity. He said it because it is true, and because the alternative, pretending agents are already driving sales, would have been a much harder story to defend on a call where Amazon's own ad revenue missed expectations.

## Is This Going to Change?

Almost certainly, eventually. Muse crossing 10 million users in under two months is not a small number, and Amazon would not have blocked it over privacy concerns if it did not see it as a real threat to the relationship it owns with its own customers. The fight over who controls agent access to a storefront is the actual story here, more than whether agents can shop yet. That fight will keep playing out through blocks, partnerships, and platform owned alternatives like Alexa for Shopping, long before most small agencies need a dedicated service line for it.

Watch this space, report honestly on what your own client data shows, and do not let anyone sell you or your clients a transformation that the platforms themselves are not seeing yet.
