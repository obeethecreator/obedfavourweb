Every agency owner has a version of the same inbox problem. Cold messages land constantly, almost all of them opening with a pitch before a single real sentence about the business on the other end. You read the first line, you know exactly what it is, and you archive it without finishing.

That is the entire reason most agency outreach fails before it starts. Not the offer. The order.

I run outbound for my own pipeline, and the sequence below is the actual one, not a cleaned up version written for a slide. Five touches, spread across twenty days, with exactly one point where an offer shows up at all.

## Find the Right Person Before You Write a Single Word

The sequence does not start with a message. It starts with a signal, because reaching out to a random agency with no reason is the same as reaching out to no one in particular.

Four signals actually matter. They are hiring for a content, operations, or account management role, which almost always means they are feeling a capacity squeeze before they will ever say it out loud. The owner is posting publicly about AI, margins, or capacity, which means the problem is already on their mind, not something you have to introduce. They just won a new client, which means the exact moment their delivery load jumped is the exact moment a system that protects their margin is most useful. Or they are already selling AI services to their own clients with no visible delivery behind it, which tells you they understand the value and just have not built the thing yet.

Skip anyone who shows none of these. A good sequence sent to the wrong person is still wasted, and no amount of personalization in the later touches will fix a list that was never qualified to begin with.

## The Five Touches, In Order

**Day zero: engage, do not pitch.** Comment on something they actually posted, or reply to something real in their feed. No message yet. The goal of day zero is only to exist on their radar before you ever ask for anything.

**Day two: connect, still no pitch.** Send the connection or follow with a short note that references the specific thing you engaged with two days earlier, not a generic "great content." Say nothing about what you do. This step only exists to make the next message land as familiar instead of cold.

**Day five: one real value message.** This is the only touch that should feel like it took effort. Name one specific workflow their business likely runs manually right now, based on what you can actually see about them, and attach a rough hours saved estimate if they automated it. Not a pitch. An observation specific enough that a generic template could not have produced it.

**Day twelve: the proof message.** Share one real result, your own build or a client's, that backs up the observation from day five. This is the first time your actual capability shows up in the conversation, and it shows up as evidence, not as an ask.

**Day twenty: close the loop.** A short, direct message that names the gap plainly and invites a short call to look at it together, no pressure attached. By day twenty, if the first four touches landed, this does not read as cold at all. It reads like a conversation that was always going to end here.

## The Pace Matters More Than the Script

Start at ten new prospects a day. Not fifty, not a hundred, ten. At ten a day you can actually personalize the day five and day twelve messages instead of running a mail merge with someone's first name dropped in, and a personalized message at ten a day will outperform a generic one at a hundred a day every single time.

Only raise the number after two clean weeks of real replies at that pace. Two clean weeks of silence is not a reason to send more messages faster. It is a reason to look hard at the signal you are targeting, the day five observation you are writing, or both.

One small habit that quietly matters more than it should: skip the em dash in your DMs. It is a tiny thing, but a message full of them reads like it came out of a template a thousand other people are also sending this week, and agency owners who run their own outreach notice that faster than almost anyone else you will ever message.

## What a Good Day Five Message Actually Looks Like

Not this: "Hi, I help agencies automate their operations. Would love to chat about how I can help you scale."

Something closer to this: "Saw you posted about taking on two new retainers this month. Most agencies I talk to at that size are still building client reports by hand, which is usually six to eight hours a month per account that never shows up on an invoice. Curious whether that is already automated for you or still manual."

The first version could have been sent to anyone. The second version proves you looked, names a specific cost in hours instead of a vague benefit, and ends in a question instead of a pitch, which is the easiest kind of message to actually answer.

## What Day Twenty Actually Sounds Like

By the time day twenty arrives, the close does not need to work hard, because the first four touches already did the work. It reads something closer to this: "Based on what you shared, it sounds like reporting is still eating a real chunk of your team's week each month. I built the exact system for this for my own pipeline and walked a couple of agencies your size through the same build. Worth a short call to see if it is worth your time too?"

Notice what is missing. No price. No feature list. No urgency manufactured out of nowhere. Just a plain restatement of the gap the prospect already told you about, tied to a result you can actually back up, ending in a low pressure invitation they can say no to without feeling chased. If the first four touches landed, most of the prospects who were ever going to say yes will say yes to exactly this.

## What to Actually Track

One pipeline is still the rule here, not five spreadsheets drifting out of sync. Track four numbers per batch of ten: how many engaged at day zero, how many replied to the day five value message specifically, how many booked a call off the day twenty close, and how long the whole sequence took from first touch to booked call.

The day five reply rate is the one number that tells you the most. If almost nobody replies to the value message, the problem is rarely the outreach channel. It is usually that the observation in that message is not specific enough, or the signal you used to pick the prospect in the first place was too loose. Fix that one message before you touch anything else in the sequence, because every later step depends on it landing.

## Where This Sequence Breaks Down

The most common failure is collapsing the five touches into two, usually by combining the connect and the value message because five days feels slow. It is not slow. It is the gap that makes the value message land as something other than a stranger asking for a favor.

The second failure is treating day twelve's proof message as optional when you do not have a flashy result yet. You do not need a huge number. One real workflow you built, for yourself or for one client, with one honest hours saved estimate, beats a vague claim about expertise every time. If you genuinely have no proof point yet, that is a sign to build one small thing for your own business before you run this sequence at all, because the sequence depends on it.

The third failure is giving up at day twenty because a specific prospect went quiet. Most will. The sequence is not built to convert everyone it touches. It is built to convert the small number who were already feeling the problem, and to do it without burning the rest of the list on a pitch they were never going to take.
