A month ago, Google pulled the "show more" button off AI Overviews for a chunk of searches. The full AI answer just loads now, automatically, pushing your organic listing further down a page most people never scroll to. That story is not breaking news anymore. What matters is it never got walked back. Google's own September Webmaster Report confirms AI Mode is still running, still expanding into more query types, and still powered by Gemini 3.7 Flash. This was not a test. This is the new default.

I have been doing SEO and growth work for close to a decade, and I have watched a lot of "Google updates" come and go. Most of them are noise. This one is structural. If you built your customer pipeline on organic search, you need to know what changed and what still works, because pretending this is temporary is how businesses quietly lose their best acquisition channel without ever seeing the moment it happened.

## What Actually Changed With Google's AI Overviews?

Here is the mechanic, stripped of jargon. AI Overviews used to show a short summary with a "show more" button underneath. You clicked it for the full answer. For a growing set of queries, Google removed that button entirely. The full AI generated answer loads by default, the follow up box already open, and your organic result sits below all of that, sometimes off screen on mobile.

Google framed it as a quality decision, saying the expansion happens "for topics where our systems determine it's most useful for people." Translate that. Google decided, on your behalf and your customer's behalf, that the answer lives on Google's page now, not on yours. The September Webmaster Report adds two more points worth knowing. Google rolled out new google.com/goto tracking redirects across search results, which protects Google's own attribution data, not yours. And AI Mode picked up new capabilities like flight tracking and hotel booking cards. This is not slowing down. It is expanding into more categories every month.

## How Bad Is Zero Click Search Right Now?

This is the part founders need to sit with. SparkToro ran a clickstream study across January through April 2026 on US search behavior, and the number came back at 68.01% zero click. For every 100 searches on Google, only 32 end with someone actually clicking through to a website. Compare that to 2024, when zero click sat at 60.45%. Compare that to 2016, when it was around 45%. Zero click search did not spike overnight. It has been climbing for a decade, and AI Overviews just put a foot on the accelerator. Thirty two out of a hundred is not a dip in your traffic. It is the ceiling most businesses are now operating under, whether they have noticed it in their analytics yet or not.

## Why Does This Matter If You Are Relying on Organic Search for Customers?

If your business model assumes someone Googles a problem, lands on your blog post or product page, and converts, that assumption is weaker than it was two years ago. Google is answering the question before your page ever gets seen. You can rank number one and still lose the click, because the AI answer already gave the searcher what they needed. Impressions go up. Visits go flat. That gap between Search Console and your analytics is the zero click tax, and it keeps getting bigger.

But here is what most panic content on this topic leaves out. Not every search behaves the same way. SparkToro's data and Google's own behavior point to the same exception: branded, local, and high intent transactional queries still convert to clicks. Someone searching your company name is not looking for a summary, they are looking for you. Someone searching "plumber near me" or "best CRM for a five person sales team" is already past research and wants a destination, not an answer. AI Overviews eats the informational, top of funnel searches first. The bottom of funnel searches are still worth fighting for.

## So What Do You Actually Do About It?

I am not going to tell you to abandon SEO, because that is lazy advice. It comes down to three moves.

First, build demand for your brand name specifically, not just your category. If people are typing your company into Google instead of a generic phrase, you sidestep most of the zero click problem, because branded search still sends clicks. Your content, your YouTube, your LinkedIn, all of it should be doing double duty as brand building, not just keyword targeting.

Second, stop treating Google as your only distribution channel. Email lists, communities, YouTube subscribers, anywhere you own the relationship instead of renting it, becomes more valuable every quarter this trend continues. The businesses getting hurt worst are the ones with no channel outside organic search.

Third, go hard after the high intent, bottom of funnel queries instead of trying to win every broad informational search. You are not going to out muscle an AI answer on "what is inventory management." You can absolutely win "best inventory management software for a 10 person warehouse team," because that searcher wants a real page, a real comparison, a real next step.

This is not the end of organic search. It is the end of organic search being easy. The businesses that treat this as permanent will be fine. The ones waiting for Google to reverse course will keep watching their traffic graphs flatten and wonder why.

## FAQ

### What is zero click search?

Zero click search is when someone searches on Google and gets their answer directly on the results page, through an AI Overview, a featured snippet, or another on page element, without clicking through to a website. SparkToro's 2026 data puts the US zero click rate at 68.01%, meaning roughly two out of every three Google searches now end without a visit to any external site.

### Did Google's AI Overviews changes kill SEO?

No, but they changed what SEO wins. Informational, top of funnel searches are increasingly answered inside AI Overviews, so ranking for those terms matters less for driving traffic. Branded, local, and high intent transactional search still reliably send clicks, so SEO focused there still works, and arguably matters more now.

### Which types of searches still convert to clicks after AI Overviews?

Branded searches, where someone is looking for a specific company by name. Local searches with clear geographic intent. High intent transactional or comparison searches where the person is ready to buy. These are the queries where an AI summary cannot substitute for actually landing on a page.

### How should a small business respond to falling organic traffic from AI Overviews?

Focus content and SEO effort on high intent, bottom of funnel searches rather than broad informational ones. Invest in building brand recognition so people search for your company by name. And build at least one distribution channel you fully own, like an email list or a YouTube audience, so your pipeline is not entirely dependent on how Google displays its results.
