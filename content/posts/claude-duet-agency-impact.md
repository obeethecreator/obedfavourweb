Anthropic unveiled something this week that agency owners serving small businesses should actually read past the headline on. On October 21, it opened a waitlisted preview of Claude Duet, a standalone AI agent that does not wait for you to open a chat and ask it something. It checks your connected accounts on its own, sends you a morning briefing, and, with your approval, can go make an actual purchase on your behalf through Stripe or Shopify.

That last part is the one worth sitting with. This is not another chatbot. This is Anthropic building a proactive assistant that can act in the exact territory agencies have been quietly monetizing for years: watching a business's accounts, flagging what needs attention, and handling the small operational tasks an owner does not have time for.

Here is what that actually changes, not the feature list.

## What Actually Happened

Duet runs as its own app on phones and the web, separate from the regular Claude app during this preview, built on Anthropic's Opus 5.5 model. It is aimed squarely at professionals and small business owners, not general consumers. It checks connected accounts every few hours on its own schedule and messages you when it decides something needs a look, rather than sitting silent until you ask.

It carries over the connectors from a user's existing Claude account, and Anthropic is pointing at more than six thousand of them in its marketplace, with named examples including Gmail, Slack, QuickBooks, Stripe, Gusto, LinkedIn, DocuSign, HubSpot, Canva, and Shopify. For a small business owner, that is most of the stack they already run on. And it can go further than reading those accounts: with approval, it can make real purchases through Stripe's Link wallet or Shopify's Shop Pay.

Anthropic has clearly thought about the obvious risk here. Duet has to show its proposed action and wait for a human to approve anything that sends a message, moves money, signs a document, or deletes something. Trading securities and moving money between accounts is blocked outright. It is still a research preview, limited to US subscribers on the Pro and Max plans, and Anthropic itself says it can misjudge urgency and is weaker on personal errands than business ones.

This is also a different move than the two other big agent launches agency owners have been reading about this autumn. OpenAI's Dots and Meta's Muse both live inside tools a business already has open, Slack, Teams, the social accounts a small business runs. Duet is making a different bet: a single standalone agent that checks everything on its own schedule and acts across all of it, rather than a feature bolted onto one tool. If that bet works, it is the clearest sign yet that the AI labs themselves want to own the proactive monitoring layer directly, not leave it to the tools built on top of their models, which is exactly the layer a lot of agency retainers currently live in.

## What This Actually Changes For Agencies

Think about what a small business client already pays an agency, or an assistant, or themselves at two in the morning to do. Watching the inbox. Checking whether an invoice needs paying. Noticing that a form filled up or a tool ran out of credits. Flagging it before it becomes a problem instead of after. That is exactly the category of work Duet is built to absorb, for the price of a subscription the client already has.

That does not make your agency obsolete. It makes the part of your retainer that is purely administrative monitoring a much easier thing for a client to question paying for, because their own AI assistant is about to start doing a version of it on its own. If a meaningful slice of what you bill for is "checking things and telling the owner what to do about it," a general purpose agent built by one of the largest AI labs on earth just got a lot closer to doing that natively, inside tools the client already uses.

What does not get replaced is the part Duet explicitly cannot do yet and may never be built to do well: understanding a specific business's actual delivery work well enough to build and run the systems behind it, not just monitor the accounts in front of it. Client reporting built around one agency's exact data sources and one client's exact story. A review response system tuned to one brand's actual voice and actual complaint patterns. A lead intake pipeline that scores and routes the way one specific sales process actually works. None of that is a connector Duet plugs into. It is a build, and a build still needs someone who understands both the automation and the business well enough to make the two fit.

## What an Agency Should Actually Do About This This Month

Look honestly at what you are charging for inside your own retainers right now. If a real slice of it is generic monitoring and basic task handling that a tool like Duet could plausibly absorb within the next year, that is worth knowing now, not when a client asks why they need you when their own assistant does the same thing. The agencies that are fine are the ones whose value was always the specific, built, supervised system, not the generic watching, and the fix is not panic, it is an honest look at your own service list against what a tool like this can actually do today, not what the headline implies it can do.
