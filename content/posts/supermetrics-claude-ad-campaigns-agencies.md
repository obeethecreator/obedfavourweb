Supermetrics just gave Claude the keys to your ad accounts. Not the reporting dashboard, the actual campaigns: Google Ads, Meta, Microsoft Advertising, TikTok, LinkedIn, and Snapchat, all buildable from a chat window as of November 3, 2026.

If you run a small agency, your first reaction is probably relief. One more login screen you do not have to touch. Your second reaction should be a question: who is checking this before it goes live?

Supermetrics already answered that one, and it is the actual story here, not the automation itself.

## What Changed, Specifically

Up to now, Supermetrics for Claude was a reporting tool. You could ask it questions about your numbers across every platform you connect and get an answer pulled straight from the source, instead of exporting six spreadsheets and stitching them together yourself at eleven at night.

As of this month, that same connection can write, not just read. You can ask Claude to build a new campaign on a given platform, and it goes and does it: ad sets, targeting, the structural work that used to eat an afternoon per platform when you were launching the same campaign everywhere at once.

The company is reporting fast growth on the reporting side already, more than six thousand customers using the connector since it launched in February, with active use climbing month over month. Take that number the way you should take any growth figure a vendor puts in its own announcement: directionally interesting, not independently verified. What matters more is the shape of what they built, not whose numbers you trust.

## The Part That Actually Matters

Here is what I noticed before anything else: new campaigns do not go live automatically. They are paused by default, and you have to actively turn them on. Edits to campaigns that are already running can be reviewed before they apply, and you can set how strict that review needs to be per account.

That is not a small feature. That is the entire product decision that determines whether this tool is useful or dangerous.

I build approval gates into every automated system I run, and I did not start doing that because I am cautious by nature. I did it because I watched a draft come back from my own content pipeline with a specific dollar figure attached to a client I have never had. Every number in that sentence was real. It just belonged to a story the model invented to sound confident. A human reading it before it went anywhere caught it in about four seconds. Nothing downstream would have caught it at all.

An ad campaign is the same risk with real money attached instead of a sentence. A model confidently wrong about audience targeting or budget pacing does not announce itself as wrong. It just spends. Supermetrics building the pause into the product by default means somebody on their team already understands this. That is the detail that tells you whether a tool was built by people who have actually shipped automation that touches client money, or people who built a demo.

## What This Actually Changes For a Small Agency

It does not change whether you need a media buyer. It changes how much of that person's day goes into mechanical setup versus actual strategy and client judgment.

Building the same campaign structure across six platforms by hand is the kind of work that eats hours without requiring much thought once you know the account. That is exactly the work worth handing to a system, as long as a person is the one who presses go. The thinking work, what to test, how to read the first week of results, when to kill something that is not working, stays exactly where it was. No tool announced this month changes that.

The risk sits with whoever turns the approval setting off because reviewing every campaign feels slow. It will feel slow for about two weeks. Then it will feel normal, the same way checking a lead score before a sales call feels normal once you have done it a hundred times. The agencies that skip the review step entirely are betting that a model will never be confidently wrong about their client's ad spend. I would not take that bet, and I build these systems for a living.

## What To Actually Do This Week

Pick one account you already run through Supermetrics, or a platform like it, and turn the review setting all the way up before you touch anything else. Build one campaign with it. Read the output the way you would read a junior hire's first draft, not the way you would read a finished deliverable. If it is clean, loosen the review slightly and watch what changes. If it is not clean, you just found out for five dollars of time what it would have cost you to find out with a client's ad budget live.

The tools are going to keep getting faster at the mechanical work. The agencies that win are not the ones that adopt fastest. They are the ones that keep a human in the loop on purpose, long after it stops feeling necessary.

Has anyone already tried the write access on a live account? I would genuinely like to hear what the first campaign it built actually looked like before you touched it.
